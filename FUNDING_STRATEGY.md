# Genesis funding strategy

## Mission
Build a sustainable, transparent funding base for Genesis so compute, storage, research, and development can grow without relying indefinitely on the Director's personal funds.

## Current position
Genesis is an early-stage, public GitHub artificial-life experiment. Its current model is a small Python simulation with a daily GitHub Actions workflow. Funding requests must describe the project honestly at this stage; do not present it as a mature research platform or claim results that have not been independently reproduced.

## Funding priorities
1. **In-kind compute credits:** Seek cloud and developer programmes that provide credits rather than cash. This is the first priority because it directly reduces infrastructure costs.
2. **Research collaboration:** Identify university labs, artificial-life researchers, computational biology groups, and evolutionary-computation communities that may offer advice, collaboration, student projects, or institutional hosting.
3. **Non-dilutive grants:** Track relevant open-science, research, education, AI safety, and digital-science grants. Check eligibility, geography, applicant type, deadlines, and permitted costs before recommending an application.
4. **Sponsorship and donations:** Once the project has a clear public roadmap, reproducible results, and transparent costs, approach aligned organisations and invite community support.
5. **Ethical earned revenue:** Consider educational material, workshops, hosted experiment dashboards, or research tools. Keep the core scientific methods and results open where practical, and disclose conflicts of interest.

## Immediate work before asking for money
- Publish a concise project overview, research question, roadmap, and honest current limitations.
- Make experiments reproducible with recorded configuration, random seed, code revision, and test results.
- Estimate monthly compute, storage, API, and domain costs.
- Keep a public funding and expenditure ledger.
- Prepare a short sponsorship / research-collaboration brief and a longer grant-ready proposal only when the evidence supports it.

## Funding opportunity assessment
For every opportunity, record:
- Programme and official source URL
- Type and value of support (cash or in-kind credits)
- Applicant eligibility and geographic restrictions
- Eligible expenses and obligations
- Deadline and application effort
- Why Genesis is a fit, and what evidence is missing
- Date last verified and next action

Do not invent programmes, award amounts, deadlines, eligibility, partnerships, or submitted applications. Re-check official sources before any application.

## Financial controls
- The Director approves all applications, contracts, spending, and commitments.
- AI research assistants may research, compare, draft, and prepare materials, but must not claim to personally contribute funds or make financial commitments.
- Prefer credits, free tiers, and collaborations before paid infrastructure.
- Set usage budgets and alerts for paid APIs and compute.
- Never publish API keys, account credentials, or private financial information.

## First funding milestone
Secure enough verified in-kind compute or research support to run a modest, reproducible pilot without requiring the Director to increase personal spending. Expand infrastructure only when the scientific need and budget are demonstrated.
